# RSNA Knee: next score experiment, 12 October 2026

## Decision

Investigate **report-to-image OA label disagreement first**, then build one **compartment-specific, cross-plane cartilage reader** for lateral and patellofemoral OA only if the supervision and anatomy pilot support it. Keep the existing readers and all 12 outputs. Treat synovitis as a separate label/sequence investigation. Do not infer a leaderboard gain from the reused 58 expert cases.

The target is a new source of evidence: physically localized cartilage morphology and lesion continuity. The scored compact model already has full-field and compartment crops, per-class spatial attention, a learned OA extent proxy, report-state heads, and 3.5 mm physical slice sampling. Repeating these features with another generic backbone is a weak hypothesis. The proposed reader differs by observing the cartilage surface in a consistent compartment coordinate frame across series and measuring how much surface has high-grade loss. It is still experimental.

The challenge asks for OA when **more than half the cartilage thickness** is lost over a **moderate or large area, roughly 1 cm or greater**, separately for medial, lateral and patellofemoral compartments. Our K extent term is a window-level learned proxy, not a direct cartilage-thickness or defect-area measurement. This mismatch makes a cartilage-specific geometric reader the clearest untested hypothesis. The organizer also defines borderline cases as negative; training labels must respect that rule.

## Ranked experiments

### 1. Report-to-image OA mismatch: fastest and potentially most consequential

**Finding:** The weakest OA ranking errors are concentrated in a few expert-positive studies that the report labelers score as negative. One report explicitly says cartilage is normal. The expert labels come from image review; we cannot declare either source incorrect by inspecting text alone. The pattern is strong enough to test the supervision path before training another reader.

**CPU audit:** Compare each available OA label source with the organizer's high-grade, large-area definition and record report states and language. Quantify missing mentions, explicit negative statements, low-grade disease, severe disease without size, and incompatible or untranslated reports in training. Check whether patient duplicates and reports from a different time point or knee could explain disagreement. Keep the reused expert cases out of threshold fitting. Do not collapse “not mentioned” into a true negative.

**Completed portion:** The five available label tables have been aligned to the 58 expert IDs and nonexpert training rows. The Yunus table was excluded from expert-case comparisons because it copies Gold exactly. Among the remaining labelers, the worst Lateral OA case is at the 9th percentile for Steven and 21st for Pilkwang against nonexpert training rows, but roughly the 48th for JEV, whose baseline scale is much lower. These sources cannot be blended as interchangeable probabilities. A [24-study non-Gold image QA cohort](image_pilot_cohort.csv) now covers high image-OOF/report-negative, high image-OOF/report-unmentioned, and low image-OOF/report-positive cases for both OA classes. Its selection is for anatomy and ambiguity inspection, not accuracy estimation.

**Controlled model test:** If label-state files permit it, train *two identical, small OA heads* under the same patient-grouped folds and compute: current targets versus an explicitly specified masked/ordinal target policy. Use an encoder whose training for a fold excludes that fold's validation cases (for example the saved fold-specific Phase 5 checkpoints); the all-data compact checkpoint cannot produce an independent OOF comparison by merely cross-validating its head. Mask uncertain/no-detail cases; preserve high-grade-large as strong positive; keep low-grade negative according to the organizer; do not silently relabel difficult cases with current model predictions. Evaluate on held-out groups and preserve both arms. This tests supervision without bundling a new architecture. Weak-report OOF will favor its own label definition, so improvement there is necessary but not sufficient. A small independent image-adjudicated cohort would resolve this uncertainty better than more public-leaderboard weight trials. Without such labels, a single fixed Kaggle evaluation can be exploratory only.

The previous small grade-supervision probe targeted Effusion and Baker's, used frozen RadImageNet features, and failed its advancement gates. It does not test this OA-specific label hypothesis, but it is a warning against assuming that severity parsing alone will help.

### 2. OA compartment reader: highest architecture upside, medium/high build risk

**Preflight, no new training:** Use the existing SKM-TEA segmenter and DICOM geometry on a small, prespecified training sample. Its current use is to locate the joint and patella; the stored anchor is not a surface measurement. Check left/right mapping, cross-series physical alignment, coverage of lateral and patellofemoral cartilage, mask continuity on adjacent slices, and whether high-grade defects cause segmentation failure. Record failures by sequence and scanner; no silent center fallback. If cartilage boundaries are unstable, abandon direct thickness estimation and use a wider ROI lesion classifier.

**Model test:** For lateral OA, read lateral femoral and tibial cartilage in sagittal and coronal series; for PF OA, read patellar and trochlear cartilage in axial and sagittal series. Derive crops from masks and patient-coordinate geometry, retain native in-plane resolution where possible, and aggregate consecutive-slice lesion evidence in millimetres. Keep separate series encoders; align features at common anatomical coordinates rather than fusing different sequence intensities into one pseudo-volume. Predict only these two OA targets in the first pilot. Use a global context branch to distinguish true defects from partial volume/artifact. Matched control: same image budget, backbone and labels with the cross-plane geometry/lesion aggregation disabled. Neither control nor experimental reader sees Gold58 during training or selection.

**Labels:** Reuse the chosen fixed policy from experiment 1, or hold the current policy fixed if experiment 1 has no support. The existing report states are `normal`, `low_grade_chondral`, `chondral_unspecified`, `high_grade_size_unstated`, `high_grade_large`, and `no_cartilage_detail`. A missing mention must remain unknown, and low-grade signal must not become the challenge's high-grade positive. Do not compare raw scores across labelers with different scales. Keep label-source changes separate from the architecture comparison.

**Evaluation gate:** Fixed patient-grouped fivefold OOF on training data, with all report/duplicate membership excluded across folds and the same folds/control input. Report both OA class AUCs, all 12-class blend AUC when aligned incumbent OOF is genuinely out of sample, pair corrections/regressions, and subgroup results by sequence availability and laterality. Weak-report OOF is a proxy. Reused Gold58 is diagnostics only; it cannot set weights. Require a positive lower bound on a paired group-bootstrap OA OOF difference, stable direction in at least four folds, and no clear regression in complete-versus-incomplete acquisition strata before full inference. A positive weak-label result alone does not establish clinical gain.

**Resource gate:** Benchmark 20 real studies for decode, segmentation, two-class inference, GPU memory and serialized output first. Scale measured per-study p95 runtime to the hidden workload; leave a safety margin under the competition's 9-hour GPU limit. Train one fold only for a feasibility/learning signal before allocating the remaining folds. Reuse existing encoded data where compatible; keep N02 and C checkpoints intact.

### 3. Synovitis: label and sequence audit before model training

This is another major unanimous-error class, but noncontrast MRI often conflates fluid and synovial thickening. First compare report state definitions and image evidence for `synovitis`, `synovial_thickening`, `fat_pad_inflammation`, and effusion; explicitly measure whether the relevant fluid-sensitive and any contrast series are present. Test a two-head **effusion versus synovial thickening** reader only if labels can distinguish them and the necessary sequences exist. Do not infer an accuracy ceiling or promise a gain from the diagnostic cases.

### 4. Low-level acquisition and geometry QA: cheap and worth doing now

Audit slice order from DICOM position/orientation, physical spacing, aspect ratio, laterality, series type, and missing/corrupt sequence handling. Current metadata completeness is associated with lower ranking concordance in the 58-case diagnostic, but case mix may explain the association. Test an exact decoded-pixel/geometry comparison before changing preprocessing. Preserve original predictions and one study-to-series audit record per run. Treat any repair as a single controlled change.

### 5. Fracture blend safeguard: narrowly scoped

Compact added only five corrected fracture pairs but thirteen regressions versus CoAtNet in the reused diagnostic. Preserve the CoAtNet fracture prediction as a fixed control and compare any new source against it; avoid a new generic model or tuned class weight based on those 58 studies.

## Score and stopping rule

At a 0.954 public baseline, reaching 0.960 requires +0.006 macro AUC, equal to +0.072 in the sum of the twelve class AUCs. For example, +0.036 for each of two OA classes would suffice **if** the gains transferred to the hidden set and all other classes stayed unchanged. This is arithmetic, not an expected result. If Helios scores 0.956 on our account, the corresponding gap to 0.960 becomes +0.004 macro. The eventual top-200/300 threshold is not established by this research.

Reject this branch after a failed anatomy/compute preflight, a matched-control loss on the weak OOF folds, or absence of any complementary effect with properly out-of-sample incumbent predictions. Do not use public leaderboard trials to search weights. If evidence survives, package one fixed candidate, run full offline inference checks, and request a single competition evaluation decision.

## Research basis

- [RSNA challenge definitions and macro AUC](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/overview/evaluation); [organizer's anatomy and label criteria](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/733343).
- [Multicentre nine-abnormality knee MRI study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12508577/): anatomy localization, multi-plane classifiers and external-centre variation. This supports the design pattern, not a transferable score.
- [Automatic knee cartilage segmentation and OA measurements](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9206408/): feasibility of cartilage thickness and joint-space biomarkers in a different MRI cohort.
- [3D cartilage/meniscus lesion classification](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6521715/): tissue segmentation followed by a lesion classifier; different source population and target.
- [Full-knee lesion grading with anatomy-specific 3D classifiers](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.2021200165): a related segmentation/detection/grading design, again not a score transferable to RSNA 2026.
- [CheXpert uncertainty-label study](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/3834): report uncertainty needs class-specific handling; this is a label-design analogy from chest imaging.
- [Noncontrast synovitis assessment limits](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6504589/): fluid and inflamed synovium can share signal without contrast.
- [DICOM image-plane position/orientation mapping](https://dicom.nema.org/dicom/2013/output/chtml/part03/sect_c.7.html): physical coordinates are needed for reliable cross-series alignment.

Its label-repair audit likewise reports no MRI AUC. Therefore a new training run should test a **measured compartment lesion signal** against the existing spatial/extent heads and this public weak-local-evidence design, rather than repackaging anatomy crops as a novel predictor. The public asset dataset reports license `other`, so checkpoint or code reuse in a competition candidate needs a separate eligibility/provenance check. The geometry pilot is only an engineering gate and does not indicate a score gain.
- The underlying [official SKM-TEA model repository](https://huggingface.co/arjundd/skm-tea-models) is publicly accessible and marks its models Apache 2.0. Its source domain is sagittal qDESS. The private [six-study mask pilot](kaggle_cartilage_mask_pilot/protocol.json) tested transfer to RSNA fluid-sensitive scans. V1 stopped after four studies on an unsupported sagittal orientation. V2 selected the longest supported series, recorded one study with no compatible series, and [completed](kaggle_cartilage_mask_pilot/output/cartilage_mask_pilot/receipt.json) in about a minute. The frozen [gate analysis](kaggle_cartilage_mask_pilot/analysis.json) **failed**: femoral cartilage met its coverage/stability requirements (5/6 studies with at least three positive slices), tibial coverage was 4/6 and median adjacent-slice Dice 0.381 (<0.4), and patellar coverage was only 2/6 with median intensity-perturbation change 0.203 (>0.1). None of the three selected PF OA studies had a patellar mask. This is a technical transfer failure, not a clinical accuracy measurement; it rules out training the proposed segmentation-derived OA views with these masks. No new classifier, AUC, or competition submission resulted.
